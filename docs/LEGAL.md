# Legal notes

These are working rules, not legal advice. Ask a lawyer before you rely on them.

## Mechanics versus expression

- Rules and ideas (how blocks clear, how a tower defence wave works) are generally not protected by copyright. Many games share them.
- Expression is protected: names, characters, art, sound, music, text, level layouts, UI layout and the overall look and feel.
- So: reimplement the rules, never copy the expression. Pick our own names, art and levels.

## Trademarks

Do not put another company's name or logo in an app name, icon or listing in a way that suggests affiliation. This includes exam brands such as CCNA, Cisco and CompTIA Security+. Do not use exam questions or "dumps".

## Books and course material

- Owning a copy is not a licence to republish, translate or build content from it.
- Facts and concepts are free to use. The author's wording, diagrams, examples and questions are not.
- Allowed use of a book: a private reference to check a fact. Then write the explanation in our own words.
- E-books often use DRM. Removing it can be illegal in some countries.

## Sources to review before using

These are candidates, **not yet checked**. Confirm each licence on the source's own page.

- NIST publications
- IETF RFCs
- MITRE ATT&CK
- OWASP (some material is share-alike: the licence may require derivative work to use the same licence)

## Assets and models

- Check the licence of every asset pack and every model before shipping its output. Some open models and datasets are non-commercial.
- Record the source and licence of each asset next to it.

## App stores

- Payment rules differ by store and country. Donation and tip links inside an app have led to removals on Google Play. Check the current policy text before adding any.
- Closed-testing requirements for new developer accounts change; check the current Play Console help.
